  <section class="panel notes" aria-label="What is real and what is modeled">
    <h2>What's real here, and what's scaled down</h2>
    <p><b>Real:</b> the chain tip and its parents, the four pools and their 1-week share of blocks, network hashrate, difficulty and nBits. The 28 starting transactions are real mempool transactions, carried as their raw bytes with the coins they spend. Your browser recomputes every txid and verifies every signature: legacy and SegWit ECDSA (with the legacy and BIP143 sighash) and Taproot Schnorr (BIP340/BIP341), plus the check that each public key hashes to the locking script. SHA-256 is a full implementation; the button above reproduces the real tip's block hash.</p>
    <p><b>Simulated, but real format:</b> when the real transactions are mined, simulated wallets keep sending. Each simulated transaction is a genuine P2WPKH transaction, signed with the wallet's own secp256k1 key and verified exactly like the real ones. It spends coins those wallets hold, including outputs of earlier simulated blocks. Coinbases are serialized for real, and their txids and the merkle roots are computed for real.</p>
    <p><b>The node cubes:</b> each cube is that node's own view of the UTXO set: every coin it knows about, placed by the address (locking script) that owns it and sized by its value. When a block is connected, the coins its transactions spend disappear and the new outputs appear, with a thin thread from each spent coin to each new one; the coinbase threads start at the gold mint at the top. A node on a losing fork briefly shows a different state until it reorgs.</p>
    <p><b>Network model:</b> each node keeps its own mempool and checks every transaction against its own chain tip before passing it on. Signature results are cached, as Bitcoin Core does. Double-spends follow full RBF: first seen wins unless the newcomer pays enough more. Nodes follow the branch with the most cumulative work, so forks and reorgs happen naturally and show up in each node's tower.</p>
    <p><b>Scaled down:</b> the real target needs about <span class="mono" id="realZeros">78</span> leading zero bits (roughly 6 × 10<sup>23</sup> hashes per block). Here each turn of a worker's handle is one real SHA-256d of the full 80-byte header, with the nonce limited to 0–100 for illustration (the real field runs to about 4.3 billion). When 0–100 is used up, the worker changes the extranonce in the coinbase, which gives a new merkle root, and starts again at 0. A block counts when its hash has at least the demo target of leading zero bits (5 by default; adjustable). Bigger pools turn their handle faster, in proportion to their share of hashrate. The header still carries the real nBits. Blocks hold up to 12,000 weight units instead of 4,000,000. Script-path Taproot, multisig and other script types are not verified by this page; none appear in the snapshot.</p>
    <p><b>Live data:</b> opened as a standalone .html file, the page fetches mempool.space directly and polls for new real blocks; every node then reorgs onto the real chain because it carries vastly more work. Inside the Claude desktop app, “Pull live data” uses the app's browser.</p>
